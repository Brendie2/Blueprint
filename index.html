The problem

You have valuable expertise.
But what exactly should you sell?

You know how to do the work. You have spent years learning, solving problems, and getting good at what you do. But when someone asks, “So, what do you sell?” the answer does not come easily.

I’m not sure how to package it all.
I have too many skills. Which one do I lead with?
I post on LinkedIn, but nobody enquires.
I send proposals and nothing happens.
People with less experience keep getting the opportunities.
I’m afraid of picking the wrong service and wasting months on it.

You may be trying to sell too many things at once. You may be leading with a skill buyers do not understand. Or you may be building an offer before finding out who actually needs it.

You are not short on ability. You are stuck between having valuable experience and turning it into something a buyer can understand, trust, and hire.

What staying unclear can cost you

The longer this stays unclear,
the more it can cost you.

This is not only a branding problem. It affects where you spend your time, what you say yes to, and whether the right buyers can recognize your value. Left alone, it can mean:

Another few months posting content with no clear offer behind it.
More proposals for services buyers find hard to understand, and more silence after you send them.
More comparing yourself with people who look clearer, more confident, or better positioned.
More time doing the work you can do, instead of building toward the work you want to be hired for.
More money spent on advice that gives you information but no usable decision.
Another awkward pause when someone asks, “So, what do you do?”
Why it has not worked yet

You do not have a
discipline problem.

You may have picked a niche, taken a personal-branding course, posted more consistently, asked AI for offer ideas, or waited for referrals. And the more advice you collect, the more confusing it gets. One person says choose a niche. Another says build your personal brand. Someone else says post every day, build a lead magnet, or sell a high-ticket offer. But how do you market a service when you are not sure what the service is?

Most advice starts too late.

It tells you to choose a niche, build a personal brand, or create content. But those steps assume you already know what you are selling, who needs it, and why they should hire you.

The real work happens earlier. It is the gap between what you know you can do and what a buyer can understand, value, and consider hiring you for. I call it the Market Translation Gap. Until you close it, content, branding, and proposals cannot do their job.

Is this for you?

Who this is for,
and who it is not for.

This is for you if

  • You have real skills, professional experience, or a record of delivering work.
  • You are not sure which skills to turn into a service.
  • You are unsure which buyers need what you do, or what they would hire you to solve.
  • You find it hard to explain your work in a way buyers understand.
  • You want to build your offer on your strengths and evidence of market demand.
  • You can attend a weekly session and do guided work between sessions.

This is not for you if

  • You are starting from scratch, with no skills or experience to build on yet.
  • You already have a clear offer that buyers want, and you mainly need more leads or help closing sales.
  • You want someone to run your marketing or sales for you.
  • You want a guarantee of clients, revenue, or sales within eight weeks.

You do not need a perfect niche, a finished offer, a polished personal brand, or a stack of testimonials. I would rather tell you this is not the right fit than sell you something that does not solve your problem.

One 90-minute working session each week, plus roughly 2 to 4 hours of guided work between sessions.
